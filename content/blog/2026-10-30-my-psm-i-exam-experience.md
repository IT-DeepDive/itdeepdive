On October 23rd I passed the **Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I)** exam from [Scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/) with a score of **98.8%** (79 out of 80). The single point I lost was in the Artifacts focus area.

I got into Scrum because lately I've been focusing on software development, both as a Product Owner and as a developer. I'm not a developer by background, but today, thanks to AI and vibe coding, I managed to build an internal tool that my company now uses. I've been assigned two developers for its next phase, and I'm leading this project and another piece of software. I can't share anything about the apps themselves, but working this way made me want to learn Scrum properly.

I got here thanks to a PSM course delivered by Rachael Milne of Emagine Consulting, organized by Jason O Callaghan and sponsored by [Qualcomm](https://www.qualcomm.com/). The training made the Scrum framework clear and practical, and it helped me prepare for the exam.

Alongside the course, I used two free resources from the source:
- The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html)
- The [Open Assessments](https://www.scrum.org/open-assessments) from Scrum.org

![The Scrum framework: the Product Backlog feeds Sprint Planning, which creates the Sprint Backlog; the Sprint, with a Daily Scrum every day, produces an Increment that meets the Definition of Done; Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective close each Sprint](/images/20261030-scrum-framework.png)

### PSM I Exam format
- 80 questions: multiple choice, multiple answer and true/false
- 60 minutes
- 85% required to pass
- $200 USD per attempt
- Lifetime certification, with no annual renewal fee

More information [here](https://www.scrum.org/assessments/professional-scrum-master-i-certification).

### Focus Areas
The result email breaks your score down by these areas, so they are a good checklist for your preparation:

Good Luck!
